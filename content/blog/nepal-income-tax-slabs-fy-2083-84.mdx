Budget 2083/84 (2026/27), presented by Finance Minister Dr. Swarnim Wagle on 15 Jestha 2083 (29 May 2026), delivered the largest personal income tax cut Nepal has seen in years. The separate schedules for single individuals and married couples were merged into one, the 1% band was doubled to Rs 10,00,000, and the peak rate was slashed from 39% to 29%.

This guide lays out the new FY 2083/84 slabs, shows exactly what changed versus FY 2082/83, and works through the numbers so you can see the impact on your own salary. To compute your own figure instantly, use our [Nepal Salary Tax Calculator](/tools/salary-tax-calculator) — it now defaults to FY 2083/84.

> **Provisional:** These rates are as proposed in Budget 2083/84 and apply from 1 Shrawan 2083 (about 17 July 2026). The annual **Finance Act 2083** gives them legal force. Confirm against the gazetted Act before filing.

## Income tax slabs for FY 2083/84 (all resident individuals)

For the first time, one unified schedule applies to everyone — single or married. Filing status no longer changes your slabs.

| Annual taxable income | Tax rate |
|---|---|
| Up to Rs 10,00,000 | 1% (0% for SSF / approved retirement-fund members) |
| Rs 10,00,001 – 15,00,000 | 10% |
| Rs 15,00,001 – 25,00,000 | 20% |
| Rs 25,00,001 – 40,00,000 | 27% |
| Above Rs 40,00,000 | 29% (27% base + 2% surcharge) |

The first slab is the 1% **Social Security Tax (SST)**. Employees who contribute to the **Social Security Fund (SSF)** or an approved retirement fund are exempt from it — so their first Rs 10 lakh is taxed at 0%.

## What changed vs FY 2082/83

Three structural changes drive the tax cut:

1. **Single and couple slabs merged.** Under FY 2082/83, couples enjoyed a higher first band (Rs 6,00,000 vs Rs 5,00,000 for singles). That distinction is gone — everyone now uses the same table.
2. **The 1% band doubled — Rs 5,00,000 → Rs 10,00,000.** Middle-income earners see the biggest relief, because a much larger slice of income is now taxed at just 1%.
3. **Top rate cut from 39% to 29%,** and the slab count fell from six to five. The old 30% / 36% / 39% chain no longer exists; the top band above Rs 40,00,000 is a composite 29% (27% + 2% surcharge).

Here is the FY 2082/83 schedule for comparison:

| Taxable income (single) | FY 2082/83 rate |
|---|---|
| Up to Rs 5,00,000 | 1% |
| Rs 5,00,001 – 7,00,000 | 10% |
| Rs 7,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 20% |
| Rs 10,00,001 – 20,00,000 | 30% |
| Rs 20,00,001 – 50,00,000 | 36% |
| Above Rs 50,00,000 | 39% |

### How much less will you pay?

Illustrative annual tax for a single filer, no deductions, non-SSF:

| Taxable income | FY 2082/83 | FY 2083/84 | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 10,00,000 | Rs 85,000 | **Rs 10,000** | Rs 75,000 |
| Rs 15,00,000 | Rs 2,35,000 | **Rs 60,000** | Rs 1,75,000 |
| Rs 25,00,000 | Rs 5,65,000 | **Rs 2,60,000** | Rs 3,05,000 |
| Rs 50,00,000 | Rs 14,65,000 | **Rs 9,55,000** | Rs 5,10,000 |

## Deductions still apply

The rate cut sits on top of the existing deduction regime, which is unchanged from Finance Act 2082:

- **Retirement (SSF + PF/EPF + CIT), combined:** deductible up to the *lowest* of the actual contribution, one-third of gross income, or **Rs 5,00,000**.
- **Life insurance premium:** up to **Rs 40,000** per year.
- **Health insurance premium:** up to **Rs 20,000** per year.
- **Single women's rebate:** resident single women with only employment income get a **10% rebate** on the computed tax (not available to couples).

## Worked example — mid earner

Gross annual income **Rs 20,00,000**, combined CIT + PF + SSF contribution of **Rs 5,00,000** (the statutory cap binds). Taxable income becomes **Rs 15,00,000**.

```
Rs 10,00,000 × 1%   = Rs 10,000
Rs  5,00,000 × 10%  = Rs 50,000
Total annual tax    = Rs 60,000   (Rs 5,000 / month)
```

If the same employee is an **SSF participant**, the first slab is 0% and the annual tax drops to **Rs 50,000**. Under FY 2082/83, that same Rs 15,00,000 taxable income cost **Rs 2,35,000** — the new slabs cut the bill by roughly three-quarters.

## Worked example — high earner

Taxable income **Rs 50,00,000** for a resident individual:

```
Rs 10,00,000 × 1%   = Rs 10,000
Rs  5,00,000 × 10%  = Rs 50,000
Rs 10,00,000 × 20%  = Rs 2,00,000
Rs 15,00,000 × 27%  = Rs 4,05,000
Rs 10,00,000 × 29%  = Rs 2,90,000
Total annual tax    = Rs 9,55,000  (Rs 79,583 / month)
```

Under FY 2082/83 (36% on Rs 20–50 lakh, 39% above), the same income cost **Rs 14,65,000** — a Rs 5,10,000 annual saving.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the single vs couple distinction really gone?**
Yes. All resident individuals now use one schedule where the first Rs 10,00,000 is taxed at 1%. Marital status no longer changes the slabs.

**Why is the top rate written as 27% + 2% surcharge?**
The band above Rs 40,00,000 is a composite 29% — a 27% base plus a 2% surcharge. Our calculator applies the 29% directly.

**When do these rates take effect?**
From 1 Shrawan 2083 (about 17 July 2026), for income earned in FY 2083/84. They are given legal force by the Finance Act 2083.

**Are these figures final?**
They are as proposed in the budget. Until the Finance Act 2083 is gazetted, treat them as provisional and verify with a Chartered Accountant.
